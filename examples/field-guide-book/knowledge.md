# The Backyard Forager's Field Guide

## What this is

A 240 page paperback about identifying and harvesting wild edible plants in gardens,
hedgerows, verges and city parks in temperate regions. It is written for people who
have never picked anything wild in their lives. It is also sold as a PDF with the same
content, searchable, with no print restriction.

## Who it is for

Absolute beginners. No botany background is assumed. No Latin name appears before it has
been introduced in plain words, and the index lists plants under their common names first.
A reader who already identifies plants confidently will find the first third familiar.

## The method the book is built on

The three signal rule. A plant counts as identified only when three independent features
agree: leaf shape and arrangement, stem or bark detail, and one seasonal marker such as a
flower, a seed head or a scent released when a leaf is crushed. If any of the three
disagrees, the book's instruction is to walk away and try again another day. The rule is
introduced in chapter one and every plant entry in the book is laid out against it.

## How the book is organised

Part one, sixty pages, teaches the three signal rule, how to use a hand lens, how to keep
a foraging notebook, and how to read the two page plant entries that follow.

Part two, four chapters, walks through the year. Each chapter covers only the plants that
are unmistakable in that season. Spring covers early leaf growth in hedgerows. Summer
covers flowers and the first soft fruit. Autumn covers nuts, seeds and late berries.
Winter covers bark, buds, evergreen leaves and root work.

Part three covers harvesting without damaging the plant or the patch, how much to take,
what to leave, and simple kitchen handling: washing, drying, storing and freezing.

## What the book deliberately leaves out

Fungi. Not one mushroom appears in the book. The introduction explains the decision: fungal
identification depends on features a beginner cannot photograph or describe reliably, and
the consequences of a mistake are different in kind. Readers who want mushrooms are told
plainly to learn them in person with someone who knows them.

Also left out: tropical and desert plants, coastal and saltmarsh plants, anything that
needs a permit or landowner permission in most regions, and anything with a common
poisonous look-alike that the three signal rule cannot separate.

## Safety stance

The book states three rules in bold on its first page. Never eat anything identified from a
single source. Never use taste as an identification method. Never harvest from a verge, a
sprayed field edge or a site with an unknown chemical history.

Chapter fourteen is a look-alike chapter. Each entry pairs an edible plant with the plant
most often confused with it and lists the one feature that separates them at a glance.

The book says in its own words that it is not a medical reference. Any question with a
health dimension, including allergies, medication interactions and anything eaten by a
child, is directed to a doctor or a poison information line, not to the book.

## Format and practical details

240 pages. 180 photographs and 40 line drawings. Sewn paperback that opens flat and stays
open. Printed on water resistant paper. 148 by 210 millimetres, which fits a coat pocket.
The PDF is identical in content and page layout.

First edition, published 2026. There is no second edition, no abridged edition and no
audio version.
